🛰️ Wall Street Blindspot
Finds investment opportunities Wall Street's major banks can't or won't cover — coverage gaps, spinoffs, orphaned names, sin-sector discounts, IPO aftermath.
📖 Strategy details▼

Wall Street Blindspot framework — finding investment opportunities that major banks structurally cannot or will not cover: coverage gaps, orphaned names, stigmatised sectors, complex structures.

  • Coverage gap: companies with 0–5 sell-side analysts — institutions only cover above $2B, leaving quality names persistently mispriced
  • Orphaned mid-caps: companies that fell below $500M and lost coverage — often trading at deep discounts to intrinsic value
  • Spinoff aftermath: newly independent companies facing forced selling from index funds in the first 18 months
  • Sin-sector discounts: quality companies in gambling, tobacco, fossil fuels, or defence at persistent ESG mandate discounts
  • Post-bankruptcy emergence: restructured balance sheets institutional investors can't own due to mandate restrictions
  • IPO aftermath: quality companies 18–24 months post-IPO when lockup expiry creates selling pressure
  • Key signal: the discount reason is structural or temporary, not fundamental — the business is better than the price suggests
